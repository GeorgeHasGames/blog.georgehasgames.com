A Flock camera is a type of automated license plate reader created by Flock Safety to track vehicles across cities. Some evidence suggests Flock tracks small details like bumper stickers and scratches. Information from Flock can be used to tell ICE the location of people, putting their safety at risk.Flock says there are audits that review suspicious uses of the system, but those audits have not been thoroughly tested for accuracy. Vandalism of Flock cameras has spread across multiple states, and the cameras are being removed from cities such as Austin, Texas, and El Paso, Texas.

Locations heavily affected by Flock cameras are far from the coast, such as New York, Rhode Island, and states along the Pacific. As more people grow concerned about their safety and privacy, cities are canceling Flock in their areas. As Flock loses support, the company tries to push the idea that the cameras are useful and worth keeping.

Another claim from Flock says images are only used for a week unless they are extended. But the probability of those extensions is high. A single Flock camera takes nearly 1.3 million images, and repeated snapshots of the same scenes are not impossible. Flaws in the Flock system are notable, such as the timing and duration of images, the amount of data collected, and overall safety concerns. But some factors are good. The Flock camera can track suspects across cities, and sometimes across state lines. Since Flock is an international company, the system travels through the US. The farther a suspect or convict travels, the harder it becomes to track them, but the sheer number of cameras helps make it possible.
